Screens:
1. Title: "So how do you actually spot a bad Spread?" Body: "You compare the Spread size to the stock's typical price movement — not just look at the raw number alone.".
2. Text: "A $0.05 Spread on a stock that moves $0.50 a day is very different from that same $0.05 Spread on a stock that only moves $0.10 a day.".
3. Text: "In the first case, the Spread is small relative to movement. In the second, it eats up half the entire daily range.".
4. Multiple choice. Prompt: "What should a Scalper compare the Spread against, to judge if it's too wide?"Four answer options: (A) "The stock's typical price movement/volatility" [correct], (B) "The company's total number of employees", (C) "The current time of day only", (D) "The Spread should never be compared to anything else". Reveal note: "Comparing Spread to typical movement tells you whether there's realistically enough room to profit after crossing it."
5. Scenario multiple choice. Prompt: "Stock A: Spread $0.03, typical daily range $0.60. Stock B: Spread $0.03, typical daily range $0.08. Which is the more viable Scalping candidate, all else equal?"Four answer options: (A) "Stock A — the Spread is small relative to its movement" [correct], (B) "Stock B — identical Spread means identical viability", (C) "Neither is viable due to having any Spread at all", (D) "Both are equally viable regardless of range". Reveal note: "Same raw Spread, very different story — Stock A leaves far more realistic room to profit."
6. True/False. Statement: "A wider raw Spread number always means a worse Scalping candidate, regardless of the stock's typical price movement."Two answer options "True" / "False" (correct). Reveal note: "Raw Spread size alone is misleading — it always needs to be judged relative to typical movement."
7. Fill-in-the-blank. Prompt: "Judging Spread quality means comparing it to the stock's typical ___, not looking at the raw number alone." Letter-tile input spelling "movement" (or "range"). Reveal note confirms the comparison principle.
