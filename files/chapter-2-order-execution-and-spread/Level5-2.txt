Chapter: 2 — Order Execution and Spread
Path: Scalping
Category: New Theory
Subcategory: Order & Spread Mechanics
Learning Goal: User understands how Market and Limit Orders interact directly with the Bid/Ask/Spread concepts from earlier in the chapter.
Purpose: Connecting order types to Bid/Ask means you can predict roughly what price you'll actually get — not just what type of order you're placing.

Screens:
1. Title: "Here's the connection you need to see." Body: "A Market buy order typically fills at the Ask price. A Market sell order typically fills at the Bid price.".
2. Text: "Bid $40.00, Ask $40.06. A Market buy order here would typically fill right around $40.06.".
3. Text: "A Limit buy order lets you set a price — even below the current Ask — but it may not fill if the market never reaches it.".
4. Multiple choice. Prompt: "A Market buy order on a stock with Bid $60.00, Ask $60.08 would typically fill around which price?"Four answer options: (A) "$60.08" [correct], (B) "$60.00", (C) "$60.04", (D) "It's impossible to know". Reveal note: "Market buys fill near the Ask — the seller's price."
5. Scenario multiple choice. Prompt: "A trader places a Limit buy order at $59.90 on a stock currently trading with Bid $60.00, Ask $60.08. What happens?"Four answer options: (A) "The order won't fill unless the price drops to $59.90 or lower" [correct], (B) "The order fills instantly at $60.08", (C) "The order fills instantly at $60.00", (D) "The order is automatically rejected as invalid". Reveal note: "A buy Limit order below the current price simply waits."
6. True/False. Statement: "A Market sell order typically fills near the Ask price."Two answer options "True" / "False" (correct). Reveal note: "Market sells fill near the Bid, not the Ask — mixing these up is a common early mistake."
7. Scenario multiple choice. Prompt: "A trader buys at the Ask via a Market order, then immediately sells at the Bid via another Market order. What have they effectively paid, ignoring any other costs?"Four answer options: (A) "The full Spread" [correct], (B) "Nothing at all", (C) "Double the Spread", (D) "Exactly half the Spread". Reveal note: "Buying at the Ask and selling at the Bid is exactly the round-trip Spread cost from Level 1-2, now tied to actual order types."
