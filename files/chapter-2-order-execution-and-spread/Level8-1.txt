Level: 8-1
Chapter: 2 — Order Execution and Spread
Path: Scalping
Category: New Theory
Subcategory: Order & Spread Mechanics
Learning Goal: User can define slippage as the difference between expected and actual fill price, distinct from both Spread and execution speed.
Purpose: Slippage explains a gap you'll actually see on real trades — knowing the name and cause for it means you won't mistake it for a platform error.

Screens:
1. Title: "A third factor to know: Slippage." Body: "Slippage is the difference between the price you expected and the price you actually got filled at.".
2. Text: "You place a Market order expecting to pay $50.00, but by the time it fills, the price has moved and you actually pay $50.03. That $0.03 gap is slippage.".
3. Text: "Slippage isn't the same as Spread — Spread is the Bid/Ask gap at a moment in time; Slippage is the price CHANGE that happens during execution.".
4. Multiple choice. Prompt: "What is slippage?"Four answer options: (A) "The difference between the expected fill price and the actual fill price" [correct], (B) "Another name for the Spread", (C) "A fee charged by brokers", (D) "The time it takes to place an order". Reveal note: "Slippage specifically refers to price movement during execution — not the Spread, and not a broker fee."
5. Scenario multiple choice. Prompt: "A Scalper expects to buy at $75.00 but the fast-moving stock fills their order at $75.04 instead. What caused this gap?"Four answer options: (A) "Slippage — the price moved during the brief execution window" [correct], (B) "The Spread alone accounts for the entire gap", (C) "A broker fee was silently added", (D) "This gap is impossible and indicates an error". Reveal note: "This is a textbook slippage scenario — price movement between order submission and fill."
6. True/False. Statement: "Slippage can only ever work against a trader, never in their favor."Two answer options "True" / "False" (correct). Reveal note: "Slippage can occasionally work in a trader's favor too — filling at a slightly better price than expected."
7. Fill-in-the-blank. Prompt: "The gap between the price you expected and the price you actually got filled at is called ___." Letter-tile input spelling "slippage". Reveal note confirms the term.

All other relevant info: Source — Aziz, Scalping chapter. Prerequisite: 7-2.
