Chapter: 2 — Order Execution and Spread
Path: Scalping
Category: New Theory
Subcategory: Order & Spread Mechanics
Learning Goal: User understands that execution speed and Spread size are related but separate factors.
Purpose: Mixing these two up leads to misdiagnosing why a trade went badly — keeping them separate helps you actually fix the right problem.

Screens:
1. Title: "Careful not to mix these two up." Body: "Execution speed is about TIME — how fast your order fills. Spread is about PRICE — the gap between Bid and Ask. They're related, but different.".
2. Text: "A trade could execute instantly (fast) but still cross a wide Spread (costly) — speed doesn't fix a bad Spread.".
3. Contrast example. Text: "Or a trade could have a great, tight Spread, but a slow execution means the price moved before it filled — a good Spread doesn't fix slow execution.".
4. Multiple choice. Prompt: "Which statement is accurate?"Four answer options: (A) "Fast execution and a tight Spread are two separate things that both matter" [correct], (B) "Fast execution automatically means a tight Spread", (C) "A tight Spread automatically means fast execution", (D) "Only one of the two ever matters at a time". Reveal note: "They're independent factors — a Scalper ideally wants both working in their favor."
5. Scenario multiple choice. Prompt: "A Scalper's order fills instantly, but at a price notably worse than expected due to a wide Spread. What went wrong?"Four answer options: (A) "The Spread was unfavorable, even though execution speed was fine" [correct], (B) "Execution speed was the problem, not the Spread", (C) "Nothing went wrong — this is the ideal outcome", (D) "The order type must have been a Limit Order". Reveal note: "Fast execution doesn't protect against a wide Spread."
6. True/False. Statement: "It's possible to have both slow execution AND a wide Spread on the same trade, making it doubly unfavorable."Two answer options "True" (correct) / "False". Reveal note: "These two factors can absolutely combine, which is why experienced Scalpers pay attention to both."
7. Matching exercise. Prompt: "Match each concept to what it measures." Terms: "Execution Speed", "Spread" matched to "How long until an order fills (time)" and "The gap between Bid and Ask (price)".
