Chapter: 2 — Order Execution and Spread
Path: Scalping
Category: New Theory
Subcategory: Order & Spread Mechanics
Learning Goal: User understands why the Spread is proportionally more significant for Scalping than for other trading styles.
Purpose: This is the core reason Scalping requires such careful stock selection — understanding it now explains nearly every rule the rest of this chapter teaches.

Screens:
1. Title: "Why does this hit Scalpers hardest?" Body: "Scalpers aim for small profits per trade — sometimes just a few cents. A Spread that size can wipe out the entire target.".
2. Contrast example. Text: "A Swing Trader aiming for a $5 move barely notices a $0.05 Spread. A Scalper aiming for a $0.10 move loses half of it to that same Spread.".
3. Multiple choice. Prompt: "Why does a $0.05 Spread matter more to a Scalper than to a Swing Trader?"Four answer options: (A) "Scalpers target much smaller price moves, so the same Spread takes a bigger bite out of the profit" [correct], (B) "Scalpers pay a completely different, higher Spread", (C) "Swing Traders are exempt from the Spread", (D) "It matters equally to both, with no difference". Reveal note: "The Spread itself doesn't change — but it represents a much larger percentage of a Scalper's small target profit."
4. Numeric scenario. Prompt: "A Scalper targets a $0.20 profit per share. The Spread on the stock is $0.08. Roughly what percentage of the target profit does the Spread eat up?"Four answer options: (A) "About 40%" [correct], (B) "About 4%", (C) "About 400%", (D) "0%, Spread doesn't affect profit targets". Reveal shows: "0.08 ÷ 0.20 = 40%."
5. True/False. Statement: "Scalpers should generally look for stocks with tighter (smaller) Spreads whenever possible."Two answer options "True" (correct) / "False". Reveal note: "Tighter Spreads leave more of a Scalper's small target profit intact."
6. Text: "This is a big reason Scalpers focus on highly liquid stocks — liquid stocks tend to have tighter Spreads.".
7. Scenario multiple choice. Prompt: "Why would a Scalper avoid a low-liquidity stock even if its price movement looks appealing?"Four answer options: (A) "Low-liquidity stocks often have wider Spreads, eating into small target profits" [correct], (B) "Low-liquidity stocks are illegal to trade", (C) "Liquidity has no connection to Spread size", (D) "Scalpers are required by law to avoid them". Reveal note: "Liquidity and Spread tightness are closely connected — a callback to Chapter 1, now applied to Scalping decisions."
