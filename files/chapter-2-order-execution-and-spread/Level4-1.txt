1. Text: "Let's lock in everything about the Spread so far.".
2. Numeric multiple choice. Prompt: "Bid is $67.42, Ask is $67.49. What is the Spread?"Four answer options: (A) "$0.07" [correct], (B) "$0.70", (C) "$67.42", (D) "$134.91". Reveal shows the subtraction worked out.
3. Scenario multiple choice. Prompt: "A Scalper targets a $0.15 profit. The Spread is $0.09. Is this Spread likely acceptable for this target?"Four answer options: (A) "No — it eats up 60% of the target, leaving little room" [correct], (B) "Yes — any Spread under $1.00 is always fine", (C) "Yes — Spread never affects profit targets", (D) "Not enough information exists to judge". Reveal shows: "0.09 ÷ 0.15 = 60%."
4. True/False. Statement: "A stock with a wide raw Spread number is always a worse choice than one with a narrow raw Spread number, no matter the typical price movement of either."Two answer options "True" / "False" (correct). Reveal note: "Raw Spread size means little without comparing it to typical movement."
5. Scenario multiple choice. Prompt: "A Scalper glances at a quote showing a tight Spread, but waits 15 seconds before acting. What two things from this chapter should concern them?"Four answer options: (A) "The Spread may have widened, and the delay itself works against fast execution" [correct], (B) "Nothing — Spreads never change once checked", (C) "Only that the stock might be delisted", (D) "Only that quotes are always wrong". Reveal note: "Both the shifting Spread and the general need for speed in Scalping combine here."
6. Matching exercise. Prompt: "Match each term to its correct description." Terms: "Bid", "Ask", "Spread", "Last Trade" matched to their four definitions.
