Level: 1-1
Chapter: 2 — Order Execution and Spread
Path: Scalping
Category: New Theory
Subcategory: Order & Spread Mechanics
Learning Goal: User can define Bid, Ask, and Spread and explain why they matter before discussing execution.
Purpose: Bid, Ask, and Spread are not abstract labels — they are the price language of the market, and they directly affect whether a trade is worth taking.

Screens:
1. Title: "Now let's learn the market language behind every trade." Body: "Bid, Ask, and Spread are the basic price terms you see on every quote. They tell you what buyers are offering, what sellers want, and how much gap sits.".
2. Text: "The Bid is the highest price a buyer will pay right now. The Ask is the lowest price a seller will accept right now.".
3. Text: "The Spread is the gap — Ask minus Bid. It is the price difference that helps define how much cost is built into the trade.".
4. Multiple choice. Prompt: "What is the Spread?"Four answer options: (A) "The gap between the Bid and Ask price" [correct], (B) "The total number of shares traded", (C) "A fee charged by the government", (D) "The time it takes for a trade to complete". Reveal note: "Spread = Ask minus Bid — the difference between the buyer's and seller's prices."
5. Numeric example. Text: "If the Bid is $50.00 and the Ask is $50.05, the Spread is $0.05.".
6. Fill-in-the-blank. Prompt: "If the Bid is $20.10 and the Ask is $20.14, the Spread equals $0.___."correct answer "04", reveal shows the subtraction worked out.
7. True/False. Statement: "The Ask price is always lower than the Bid price."Two answer options "True" / "False" (correct). Reveal note: "It's the reverse — Ask is the seller's price (higher), Bid is the buyer's price (lower)."
8. Scenario multiple choice. Prompt: "A stock shows Bid $100.00 and Ask $100.50. A trader buying right now would pay closer to which price?"Four answer options: (A) "$100.50, the Ask price" [correct], (B) "$100.00, the Bid price", (C) "Exactly halfway, always", (D) "Neither — price is unrelated to Bid/Ask". Reveal note: "Buyers generally pay at or near the Ask price when they want immediate execution."

All other relevant info: Source — Murphy, market microstructure basics; Aziz, Scalping chapter. Prerequisite: Chapter 1 Final Exam. First level of Chapter 2.
