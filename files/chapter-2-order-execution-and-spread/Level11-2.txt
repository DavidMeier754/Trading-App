Learning Goal: User can combine Spread, slippage, and transaction fees into a single "all-in cost per trade" figure and apply it to judge whether a target profit is realistic.
Purpose: This single number — all-in cost — is the fastest way to judge whether a potential trade is even worth attempting.

Screens:
1. Title: "Let's put all three cost layers together." Body: "Spread, slippage, and transaction fees combine into one real number: your all-in cost per trade. For clarity, these examples use the same unit throughout — per share or per trade — and the same unit must be used when comparing costs.".
2. Text: "Spread $0.05 + slippage $0.02 + fees $0.01 = $0.08 all-in cost. If your target is $0.20, costs eat 40% of it before any profit is locked in.".
3. Text: "Some experienced Scalpers calculate this all-in figure before placing a trade — it's a habit worth building early, and the unit matters just as much as the number itself.".
4. Numeric multiple choice. Prompt: "Spread is $0.04, slippage is $0.01, and fees are $0.02. What is the all-in cost per trade?"Four answer options: (A) "$0.07" [correct], (B) "$0.04", (C) "$0.10", (D) "$0.02". Reveal shows $0.04+$0.01+$0.02=$0.07.
5. Numeric scenario. Prompt: "All-in cost is $0.09. Target profit is $0.30. Roughly what percentage of the target is consumed by costs?"Four answer options: (A) "30%" [correct], (B) "9%", (C) "3%", (D) "90%". Reveal shows $0.09÷$0.30=30%.
6. True/False. Statement: "A trade with a low all-in cost relative to its target profit is generally more favorable than one with a high all-in cost relative to target."Two answer options "True" (correct) / "False". Reveal note: "Lower relative cost leaves more room for actual profit."
7. Scenario multiple choice. Prompt: "Two potential trades: Trade A has all-in cost of 15% of target profit. Trade B has all-in cost of 60% of target profit. All else being equal, which is the more attractive setup?"Four answer options: (A) "Trade A — significantly more of the target profit remains achievable" [correct], (B) "Trade B — higher percentages are always better", (C) "They are equally attractive", (D) "Neither is attractive because costs exist at all". Reveal note: "15% versus 60% is a dramatic difference — Trade A leaves far more genuine profit potential after costs."
