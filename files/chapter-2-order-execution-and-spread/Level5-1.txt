Path: Scalping
Category: New Theory
Subcategory: Order & Spread Mechanics
Learning Goal: User can restate the core difference between Market Orders and Limit Orders.
Purpose: Every trade you'll ever place uses one of these two order types — knowing the trade-off is essential before deciding which fits Scalping best.

Screens:
1. Title: "Now let's talk about how you actually place a trade." Body: "Two basic order types exist: Market Orders and Limit Orders — each behaves very differently.".
2. Text: "A Market Order is designed to execute as quickly as possible at the best currently available price — speed over exact price control.".
3. Text: "A Limit Order only executes at the price you set or better — price control over guaranteed speed.".
4. Multiple choice. Prompt: "What is the defining feature of a Market Order?"Four answer options: (A) "It executes immediately at the best available price" [correct], (B) "It only executes at a price you specify", (C) "It cancels automatically after one minute", (D) "It requires approval from a broker representative". Reveal note: "Speed and immediacy define a Market Order."
5. Multiple choice. Prompt: "What is the defining feature of a Limit Order?"Four answer options: (A) "It only executes at your specified price or better" [correct], (B) "It always executes faster than a Market Order", (C) "It guarantees the lowest possible fee", (D) "It's only available to institutional investors". Reveal note: "Price control is the trade-off — execution is not guaranteed to happen immediately, or at all."
6. True/False. Statement: "A Limit Order guarantees that your trade will execute, just possibly at a delay."Two answer options "True" / "False" (correct). Reveal note: "A Limit Order may never execute if the price you set is never reached."
7. Scenario multiple choice. Prompt: "A trader wants to guarantee getting into a trade right now, and is willing to accept whatever the current price is. Which order type fits?"Four answer options: (A) "Market Order" [correct], (B) "Limit Order", (C) "Neither order type can do this", (D) "Both are identical in this situation". Reveal note: "Wanting immediate execution regardless of exact price is exactly what a Market Order is built for."
