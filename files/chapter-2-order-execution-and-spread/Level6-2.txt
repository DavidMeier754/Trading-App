Level: 6-2
Chapter: 2 — Order Execution and Spread
Path: Scalping
Category: New Theory
Subcategory: Order & Spread Mechanics
Learning Goal: User understands a common practical approach — using Limit Orders for planned exits even when Market Orders are used for fast entries — as one option among several, not a fixed rule.
Purpose: This combination is one practical way traders balance speed and precision — knowing it gives you a real, usable pattern to consider, alongside its limitations.

Screens:
1. Title: "Entries and exits don't have to use the same order type." Body: "Some Scalpers enter fast with a Market Order, then plan their exit with a Limit Order at a specific target price. This is one common approach, not the only valid one.".
2. Text: "Enter via Market Order at $25.10. Set a Limit sell order at $25.20 — if the price gets there, the exit may execute at that price or better, depending on market conditions.".
3. Multiple choice. Prompt: "What's one practical approach some Scalpers use for entries versus exits?"Four answer options: (A) "Market Order to enter fast, Limit Order to exit at a specific target" [correct], (B) "Limit Order to enter, Market Order to exit, always", (C) "Only ever use Market Orders for both", (D) "Only ever use Limit Orders for both". Reveal note: "This combination balances speed on entry with price control on exit — though it's a common pattern, not an absolute rule."
4. Scenario multiple choice. Prompt: "A Scalper enters a position via Market Order at $30.00, hoping to exit at $30.15. Which order type lets them set $30.15 as the minimum acceptable exit price, if reached?"Four answer options: (A) "A Limit sell order at $30.15" [correct], (B) "A Market sell order", (C) "Another Market buy order", (D) "No order type can establish a target exit price". Reveal note: "A Limit sell order at $30.15 will only execute at that price or better if the market reaches it."
5. True/False. Statement: "Using a Limit Order for an exit guarantees the trade will definitely close at some point."Two answer options "True" / "False" (correct). Reveal note: "Same trade-off as always with Limit Orders: if the price never reaches $30.15, that exit order simply never fills."
6. Scenario multiple choice. Prompt: "A Scalper's Limit exit order never fills because the price reverses instead of reaching the target. What real risk does this expose them to?"Four answer options: (A) "Continuing to hold the position longer than planned, exposed to further price movement" [correct], (B) "No risk at all — the trade simply cancels itself safely", (C) "An automatic profit is generated instead", (D) "The stock is permanently frozen". Reveal note: "An unfilled Limit exit means the position stays open and exposed — a real practical risk, even though full risk-management tools come in a later chapter."

All other relevant info: Source — Aziz, Scalping chapter (softened per audit — presented as one common approach, not a rule). Prerequisite: 6-1. Deliberately does not introduce stop-losses here — that belongs to the dedicated Risk Management chapter later in the path.
