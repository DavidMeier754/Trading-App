Level: 1-3
Chapter: 1 — Market Basics
Path: Scalping (identical across all paths)
Category: Repetition
Subcategory: Repetition
Learning Goal: User reinforces and combines the ownership, market, and supply/demand concepts from 1-1 and 1-2, at slightly increased difficulty.
Purpose: These two ideas — ownership and supply/demand — reappear in almost every later chapter, so locking them in now saves confusion later.

Screens:
1. Text: "Let's make sure these first ideas really stick before we move on."
2. Numeric multiple choice. Prompt: "You buy 5 out of a company's 100 total shares. What best describes your position?"Four answer options: (A) "You own 5% of the company" [correct], (B) "You are owed 5% interest annually", (C) "You manage 5% of the company's staff", (D) "You get 5% of all future product discounts". Reveal note: "5 out of 100 shares = 5% ownership."
3. Scenario multiple choice. Prompt: "A company sells new shares to investors who want to buy in. Name one direct effect of this, based on what you've learned."Four answer options: (A) "The company raises cash, and ownership is spread across more shares" [correct], (B) "The company loses all its shares for free", (C) "Selling pressure increases while buying pressure drops", (D) "Nothing changes for the company at all". Reveal note: "New investors buying in brings cash to the company and spreads ownership across more shares — the price effect depends on supply and demand, not just on the financing itself."
4. True/False. Statement: "If a company sells more new shares to raise money, that alone always makes its stock price rise."Two answer options "True" / "False" (correct). Reveal note: "Raising money and moving the price are separate things — the price depends on buy/sell pressure, not just on the company getting cash."
5. Matching exercise. Prompt: "Match each term to its meaning." Four terms: "Buyer", "Seller", "Market", "Shareholder" matched to their definitions, including "Someone who owns part of a company" as the new pairing.
6. Numeric fill-in-the-blank. Prompt: "A stock has 80 buy orders waiting and 20 sell orders waiting. Based on the buy/sell imbalance, the price is more likely to go ___." Letter-tile input spelling "up". Reveal note: "80 buyers to 20 sellers is heavy buying pressure — the same rule as before, applied to real numbers."
7. Scenario multiple choice. Prompt: "Two companies each sell new shares. Company A sees more buyers than sellers afterward; Company B sees more sellers than buyers. What's the likely price difference?"Four answer options: (A) "Company A's price is more likely to rise; Company B's more likely to fall" [correct], (B) "Both will move in the same direction", (C) "Selling shares guarantees both prices rise equally", (D) "Price is unrelated to buyer/seller counts". Reveal note: "Same supply/demand rule, just applied to two different companies at once."

All other relevant info: Source — combines 1-1 and 1-2. Prerequisite: 1-2. Escalates via numeric framing and combined scenarios, per agent.md v5.
