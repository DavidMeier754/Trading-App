Learning Goal: User reinforces and combines the trading-style distinctions from 3-1 and 3-2, including a review of supply/demand and index concepts from earlier levels.
Purpose: This is the last checkpoint on trading styles before Chapter 1 moves into market timing — worth locking in solidly since your whole path is built around one of these three styles.

Screens:
1. Text: "Let's lock in the difference between the three styles — and connect it back to what we learned earlier.".
2. Multiple choice. Prompt: "A trader wants to minimize daily screen time as much as possible while still actively trading (not just long-term investing). Which style tends to fit best?"Four answer options: (A) "Swing Trading" [correct], (B) "Day Trading", (C) "Scalping", (D) "All three require identical time commitment". Reveal note: "Swing Trading's multi-day holding period is what generally makes it the lower-screen-time active style."
3. Scenario multiple choice. Prompt: "A Day Trader sees far more people trying to sell a stock than buy it right before market close, and closes their position immediately, as their style requires. What happens to the price, and why did they have to act before close?"Four answer options: (A) "Price likely falls due to selling pressure; Day Traders always close before the day ends" [correct], (B) "Price likely rises; Day Traders always hold overnight", (C) "Price is unaffected by buy/sell pressure", (D) "Day Traders can hold positions for weeks". Reveal note: "Two ideas at once: more sellers than buyers pushes price down, and Day Traders always close before the day ends."
4. True/False. Statement: "Scalping and Swing Trading require roughly the same amount of daily screen time."Two answer options "True" / "False" (correct). Reveal note: "These two are near-opposite ends of the screen-time spectrum."
5. Matching exercise. Prompt: "Match each style to BOTH its holding period AND its typical screen-time level."Three terms each matched to two things — six total drop-zones.
6. Multiple choice. Prompt: "A Swing Trader holding a position for two weeks checks a major index to get a quick read on 'the market' overall. What is she most likely looking at?"Four answer options: (A) "Something like the S&P 500" [correct], (B) "A single random company's stock", (C) "Her own personal bank balance", (D) "A market maker's private records". Reveal note: "Indexes like the S&P 500 are exactly the quick-glance tool traders of any style use."
