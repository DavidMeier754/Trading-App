Learning Goal: User can connect market phases to real trading behavior, understanding that different traders — including some in every style — pay attention to pre-market and after-hours for different reasons.
Purpose: Understanding why some traders watch pre-market activity helps you interpret news-driven price moves before they fully play out in regular hours.

Screens:
1. Title: "So how does this affect how you'll trade?" Body: "Many traders concentrate their activity in regular hours, when things tend to be more predictable — though some traders in every style also watch pre-market and after-hours closely.".
2. Text: "One common reason to watch pre-market or after-hours: reacting to big news before everyone else can.".
3. Multiple choice. Prompt: "Why might a trader specifically watch pre-market activity?"Four answer options: (A) "To react early to major news before regular hours begin" [correct], (B) "Because pre-market always guarantees profit", (C) "Because pre-market is required by law", (D) "Because pre-market has the lowest risk of all phases". Reveal note: "News often breaks outside regular hours — watching pre-market lets some traders respond early, though it comes with more risk, not less."
4. Scenario multiple choice. Prompt: "A company announces major news at 7 AM, two hours before regular trading begins. A trader immediately checks pre-market activity. What is she trying to do?"Four answer options: (A) "Gauge how the market is reacting to the news before regular hours start" [correct], (B) "Confirm the stock market is still open", (C) "Check if pre-market trading is legal", (D) "Wait for the news to be cancelled". Reveal note: "Pre-market gives an early read on how news might move the price once regular hours begin."
5. True/False. Statement: "Reacting to pre-market or after-hours moves is generally considered lower-risk than trading in regular hours."Two answer options "True" / "False" (correct). Reveal note: "It's the opposite — lower activity in those phases usually means MORE risk, not less."
6. Fill-in-the-blank. Prompt: "Because fewer traders are active outside regular hours, price swings during pre-market and after-hours tend to be ___ predictable than during regular hours." Letter-tile input spelling "less". Reveal note: "Less predictable — tying back to the low-activity/high-swing relationship."
7. Multiple choice. Prompt: "Based on everything so far, why might a brand-new trader want to start practicing mainly during regular hours?"Four answer options: (A) "Regular hours tend to have higher activity and more predictable price behavior" [correct], (B) "Pre-market trading is against the rules for new traders", (C) "After-hours trading requires a special license", (D) "It doesn't matter at all". Reveal note: "Regular hours are a natural starting point — more predictable, more company."

All other relevant info: Source — Aziz, Ch. 2 (extended reasoning, softened per audit to avoid overgeneralizing "most trading styles concentrate in regular hours" as an absolute, since some traders in every style — including Scalping — do watch pre-market/after-hours). Prerequisite: 4-1.
