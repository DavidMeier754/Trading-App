Level: 5-2
Chapter: 1 — Market Basics
Path: Scalping (identical across all paths)
Category: New Theory
Subcategory: Market Structure
Learning Goal: User can define volatility (magnitude and speed of price movement) and distinguish it clearly from liquidity.
Purpose: Confusing liquidity and volatility is one of the most common beginner mix-ups — keeping them straight will help you read stocks correctly for the rest of this app.

Screens:
1. Title: "A different question: how MUCH does the price move?" Body: "Volatility describes how big and how fast a stock's price swings — regardless of how easy it is to trade.".
2. Text: "A stock jumping 10% in a single day is highly volatile. A stock barely moving 0.1% is the opposite.".
3. Text: "Volatility is NOT the same as liquidity — a stock can be easy to trade (liquid) and still swing wildly (volatile), or vice versa.".
4. Multiple choice. Prompt: "What does 'high volatility' mean?"Four answer options: (A) "Big, fast price swings" [correct], (B) "A stock that's easy to buy and sell", (C) "A stock guaranteed to go up", (D) "A stock with no daily trading activity". Reveal note: "Volatility is about the SIZE and SPEED of price moves — not ease of trading, which is liquidity."
5. Scenario multiple choice. Prompt: "A stock trades millions of shares daily (easy to buy/sell) but its price barely moves day to day. How would you describe it?"Four answer options: (A) "Highly liquid, low volatility" [correct], (B) "Highly liquid, high volatility", (C) "Low liquidity, high volatility", (D) "Low liquidity, low volatility". Reveal note: "Easy to trade (liquid) and calm price movement (low volatility) can absolutely coexist."
6. True/False. Statement: "A perfectly flat, unmoving price chart represents high volatility."Two answer options "True" / "False" (correct). Reveal note: "Flat and unmoving is the definition of LOW volatility."
7. Matching exercise. Prompt: "Match each term to its correct definition." Terms: "High Liquidity", "Low Liquidity", "High Volatility", "Low Volatility" matched to four definitions.

All other relevant info: Source — Aziz, Ch. 2; Murphy, Introduction. Prerequisite: 5-1.
