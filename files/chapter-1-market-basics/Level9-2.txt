Learning Goal: User connects bull/bear market context to trading-style and volatility concepts learned earlier, understanding overall market trend as background context rather than a trading signal by itself.
Purpose: This distinction prevents a common beginner mistake — assuming the big-picture trend changes the rules of your trading style, when really it doesn't.

Screens:
1. Title: "Does the overall trend change how you trade day to day?" Body: "Bull and bear markets are background context — useful to know, but they don't replace watching individual stocks and their own price action.".
2. Text: "A stock can still be highly volatile on a single day regardless of whether the overall market is in a bull or bear phase.".
3. Multiple choice. Prompt: "Does being in a bull market guarantee a specific stock won't be volatile on any given day?"Four answer options: (A) "No — individual stocks can still swing sharply regardless of the overall trend" [correct], (B) "Yes — bull markets eliminate all volatility", (C) "Yes, but only for very large companies", (D) "This question doesn't make sense". Reveal note: "Overall trend and single-day volatility are separate things."
4. Scenario multiple choice. Prompt: "During a bear market, a Day Trader still closes every position before the trading day ends, same as always. Does the bear market change this rule?"Four answer options: (A) "No — the same-day-close rule applies regardless of overall market trend" [correct], (B) "Yes — Day Traders hold overnight during bear markets", (C) "Yes — bear markets suspend the same-day-close rule", (D) "Day Trading isn't allowed during bear markets". Reveal note: "A trading style's core rules stay the same no matter what the overall market is doing."
5. True/False. Statement: "Knowing whether it's a bull or bear market is completely useless information for a trader."Two answer options "True" / "False" (correct). Reveal note: "It's useful context — just not a replacement for watching what an individual stock is actually doing right now."
