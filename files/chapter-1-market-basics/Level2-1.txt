Level: 2-1
Chapter: 1 — Market Basics
Path: Scalping (identical across all paths)
Category: New Theory
Subcategory: Market Structure
Learning Goal: User can explain what a stock index is, why it exists, and name a real-world example.
Purpose: News and financial headlines constantly reference "the market" or major indexes — understanding what that actually means helps you make sense of the world around your trading.

Screens:
1. Title: "How do you measure 'the whole market' at once?" Body: "You can't watch every single stock — so people use an index, which tracks a group of stocks together, like a scoreboard.".
2. Text: "The S&P 500 is a well-known index that tracks 500 large U.S. companies at once.".
3. Title: "Why bother with an index?" Body: "If most of the 500 companies go up, the index goes up — giving you a quick read on 'the market' without checking each one.".
4. Multiple choice. Prompt: "What is the main purpose of a stock index?"Four answer options: (A) "To track a group of stocks together as one overall measure" [correct], (B) "To guarantee profits for everyone who buys it", (C) "To replace the need for a stock market entirely", (D) "To set legal rules for how companies can trade". Reveal note: "An index is a measuring tool, not a guarantee and not a replacement for the market itself."
5. Scenario multiple choice. Prompt: "News reports say 'the market had a strong day today.' What are they most likely referring to?"Four answer options: (A) "A major index, like the S&P 500, went up" [correct], (B) "Every single company in the world went up", (C) "A new stock market building opened", (D) "Interest rates were announced". Reveal note: "'The market' in headlines almost always means a major index."
6. True/False. Statement: "An index and a single company's stock are the same thing."Two answer options "True" / "False" (correct). Reveal note: "A single stock is one company. An index bundles many companies into one number."
7. Matching exercise. Prompt: "Match each term to what it represents." tiles: "Single Stock", "Index" matched to "One company" and "A group of companies tracked together".
8. Scenario multiple choice. Prompt: "An index made of 500 companies rises today. Does this guarantee every one of those 500 companies also rose?"Four answer options: (A) "No — the index reflects the overall group, not every single company" [correct], (B) "Yes — every company must rise for the index to rise", (C) "Only the 10 largest companies matter for this", (D) "This is impossible to determine". Reveal note: "An index can rise even if some individual companies within it fall — it reflects the overall balance."

All other relevant info: Source — Murphy, Introduction (index concept, simplified). Prerequisite: 1-3.
