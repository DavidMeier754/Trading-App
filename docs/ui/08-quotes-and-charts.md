# Quotes and charts

_Part of the [ui reference](README.md) · §6–6.4_

## 6. Visual & data components

All components are theme-aware (section 10). **Up = green, down = red**, always paired with an arrow or sign.

### 6.1 Ownership pie
Circle of N equal slices; owned slices fill one by one (80 ms each). Caption "5 of 50 = 10 %". **[v4]** Above 20 parts it is a 10 × 10 grid of squares instead (1,000 parts: a grid of hundreds), because a circle of 100 slices reads as a dark disc. **[LOOK-COMPONENTS] Built:** up to 100 parts each square is one part; past that the hundred squares share them equally (1,000 shares: ten a square, "Each square is 10 shares." under the caption), and a part of a square fills from its left. The owned squares light one after another, row by row.

### 6.2 Quote card
```
 ┌──────────────────────┐
 │ XYZ   Example Corp   │   ticker + name
 │ 142.50               │   current price (large)
 │ ▲ +2.10 (+1.5 %)     │   change vs previous close, colored, with arrow
 │ Vol 3.2 M            │   optional
 └──────────────────────┘
```
Digits flicker (100 ms) on update in live-demo mode. Every field can be a `hotspot` target or `walkthrough` spotlight.

### 6.3 Quote panel (Bid / Ask / Last / Spread)
```
   BID        SPREAD        ASK
  45.20 ──── 0.04 ────►   45.24
          LAST 45.22
```
Bid green-tinted left, Ask red-tinted right, spread as a bracketed gap with its value. **Spread animation:** the gap physically widens/narrows (300 ms) when values change. Optional depth bars below (6.6).

A tap is not a scrub, so a tap does what it did; charts that are answered on (`chart-tap`, `chart-annotate`) have none. `src/components/ChartScrub.tsx`.

Absent when the decision rests on the chart alone. **[LOOK-COMPONENTS]** Easy to read: "Day: −2R" is drawn as the name "Day", quiet, and the value "−2R" in the text colour, bold, its number in the number face; a day's result in R takes its sign's colour, the sign staying with it. 14 pt, on the surface with a strong border.
- **Decision overlay:** at the pause point the three buttons rise from the bottom; after the choice the chart continues and a P/L strip shows the outcome, then the rationale.

- **[v4] Stop and target** from the level file (`stop`, `target`) draw as labelled lines, and playback ends at the first one touched. **[DESIGN-REVIEW]** Built: they appear with the choice, not before it (they would give the direction away), with the entry as a third, quieter line; their price tags sit on the axis in the number face.
- **[DESIGN-REVIEW] The R ruler.** On a decision with `stop` and `target`, from the lesson that teaches R on (Scalping 6·2-1; the app checks that the lesson introducing the term "R" is behind the learner), a slim ruler stands beside the price axis: −1R at the stop, 0 at the entry, +1R, +2R … up to the target. While the trade plays out a marker climbs or falls along it and stops on the result, labelled in R ("+1.6R"). It shows when the learner took the trade the file describes, faint when they stood aside, and not at all when they traded the other way. It teaches R by being there every time, without a sentence.
- **[DESIGN-REVIEW] Chart notes.** After the reveal, the file's `notes` (1–4) appear on the chart, each a few words on a small tag with a thin leader line to the top or the bottom of the bar it means ("Lower high", "Breaks the shelf"). They fade in one after another once playback has ended; they never cover the last bar's price tag, and they are part of the screen reader's description.
- **[DESIGN-REVIEW] Words on the chart stay readable.** Built: every label on a chart — a level's name and price, the entry, the stop, the target — is drawn over the bars on a thin rim of the page's colour, so no candle hides a price. A level's label sits at the left end of its line, just over it; it moves to the right end, or under the line, only where that clears a real part of a bar (not the tip of a wick), judged against every bar of the chart, those still to come included, so it never moves while the replay plays. The stop's and the target's labels take the other side of their line when the usual side would run into the "decision" tag at the top. A note steps further out from its bar when it would cover a label, another note or another bar, and takes the bar's other side when its own has no room; the outcome tag steps clear of the levels' labels. On a narrow chart a long label can still cross a candle; the rim keeps both legible. **[DESIGN-REVIEW, 2026-10-04]** Labels never cover each other or leave the plot: two levels a few cents apart (Ch 4 10-1: a round number and yesterday's close, two cents apart, at the top of the chart) take a row further from their line rather than one spot; a label stays inside the plot, off the price axis, and one too long for it with its price drops the price, which the axis shows; the "next 5 candles" pill in the hatched zone moves up or down to make way for a label. The mini chart of `swipe-deck` and `compare` places its labels the same way.
- **[DESIGN-REVIEW] The open.** A chart with `session_open` shades the bars before the open as pre-market, faintly, and draws a dashed line before the first regular bar with a small bell and "Open". Only on charts where the open matters (David: "only on charts where it is important … in levels testing setups"), and explained the first time (`docs/content-todo/01-rules-from-the-design-review.md` 1.2).
- **[DESIGN-REVIEW] Simple first.** Charts start with as little on them as possible and gain information as the path goes on: a line, then candles, volume, levels, the open, stop and target, VWAP, the R ruler. Nothing appears before the lesson that explains it, and the first chart that shows it explains it (the ramp: `docs/content-todo/01-rules-from-the-design-review.md` 1.2).

#### 6.4a The "?" key **[LOOK-COMPONENTS, built 2026-10-07]**

- **Where.** A round "?" (30 pt, in a 48 pt target) in the top-right corner of the plot, over the part a decision chart keeps hatched for what comes next. **[David, 2026-10-08]** Once the outcome has played, the bars can fill that corner (a call that ran up ends there), so after the call the key takes the bottom-right corner when candles or labels would lie under a sixth of the top one and the bottom is freer; while the learner decides it is always at the top. On a `chart-decision` while the learner decides and once the outcome has played (not while it plays: then the chart is the key that skips it), on a theory card's or a `visual`'s chart, and above a scanner table, right-aligned. `chart-tap`, `chart-annotate`, `chart-replay` and the mini charts of `swipe-deck` and `compare` have none: they are answered by touching them, as they have no zoom. A chart with nothing taught on it shows no key.
- **Pressed.** Each element it labels gets a number in a small accent circle, on the element: the VWAP line at a point of it, a level and the stop's and the target's lines on the line itself, the volume strip at its left, the pill just above it, the R ruler under its lower end, a state chip on its corner, a scanner heading just above it. **[David, 2026-10-08: "the points linked to the legend are off"]** A number on a line takes the first spot along it, from its right end leftwards (on the VWAP line, from its latest point in view), that covers no label, no candle, no other number and not the key; a label's words or a candle under a number read as pointing at the wrong thing. A legend under the chart lists the numbers, each with the skill's name in bold and its one line from `content/skills.yaml`, in full. While the learner decides, the chart gives up exactly the legend's room, measured beforehand, so nothing on the chart is covered and the decision keys stay where they are. **[David, 2026-10-08]** Once the verdict shows, the legend takes the verdict's place instead: the verdict steps aside while the legend is open and is back where it was when "?" is pressed again, and the chart keeps its size (giving up the legend's room as well as the verdict's left it squeezed, and a chart that changed size would move the key from under the finger). Only a legend taller than the verdict takes the difference from the chart. On a theory card the legend opens under the chart. On a scanner it lies over the rows under the headings. Pressed again, the numbers and the legend go. The call puts it away; it can be pressed again when the outcome has played.
- **What is labelled, in reading order:** the state chips, the pill, the VWAP line, each level, the volume bars, the plan's stop and target, the R ruler. A level is named by the term its label holds ("Pre-market high" → Pre-market, "VWAP" → VWAP); a label that names none ("Floor", "Ceiling", "Round number") is Support below the last price the learner can see and Resistance above it. A chip is named by what its name stands for (Size → Position size, Day → R, Limit → Daily loss limit, Trades → Trade), else by a term its words hold; a chip that names none ("Account") gets no number. A scanner's headings: Ticker, Catalyst (with the tickers), Daily change, Relative volume, Spread, Float.
- **Taught** means as for the R ruler: the lesson that introduces the term is played, or this lesson comes after it on the path. The test bench and the lessons made in code label everything. The pill is the app's own, not a skill, so it is labelled on every decision chart, with one line of the app's: "Hidden until you make your call. Then these candles play out, and you see what happened."
- `src/components/explain.ts` (what and with which words), `src/components/ExplainKey.tsx` (the key, the numbers, the legend).
