## Why the chapter exists

Chapter 7 hands the learner finished cards and Chapter 8 a routine to run them; 7·1-3 even asks for a first card of their own. Nothing yet teaches how to turn an idea into a strategy, test it honestly and decide whether to keep it. That is also where David's rule on odds lands: "The user should do his own research on how often strats work for him" (`docs/rules/07-variance-and-typed-numbers.md` §3.11, `docs/content-todo/01-rules-from-the-design-review.md` 1.1). Until now the course only said it; this chapter teaches how.

## Shape

- **Shared by all three paths.** Folder `content/shared/chapter-09-your-own-strategy/`. It opens on the map after the path's Chapter 8 Final Exam, whichever path the learner took. Its references are to the path skeleton (Chapter 6 risk and the journal, Chapter 7 the playbook card, Chapter 8 the tracking and the simulator plan), which is the same in every path.
- **Shorter than a path chapter, on purpose.** Six levels and about 16 sub-levels, so §3.1's 15-level minimum (`docs/rules/03-content-rules.md`) does not apply to it; `docs/rules/02-paths-and-chapters.md` records the exception. Every other content rule applies.
- **No odds from us.** Every number in the chapter is either an example labelled as one or the learner's own count. No screen says how often any idea works.
- **The plan sheet.** The learner's own card goes on the plan sheet next to the eight (`plan-card`, a new slot), and comes back in the capstone.

## Levels

| Level | Title | Subs | Teaches | Reinforces |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Where Ideas Come From | 3 | Ideas from your own rows: the journal's best column, the near misses you keep noting, a shape you keep seeing. An idea is not a strategy until it is a card. One idea at a time. | 6, 7 |
| 2 | Write It as a Card | 3 | The five fields as instructions (7·1-3), plus which names, which time of day, which day type, and the size rule. The test: would two traders act in the same minute? A card that cannot be tested yet is a description. | 7 |
| 3 | Test It on the Past, Honestly | 3 | Replays bar by bar, deciding before the next bar, and a row for every trigger, the ugly ones included. Two traps: hindsight, and changing the rules until the past looks good. Rules first, then look. | 6 |
| 4 | Count It | 3 | R after costs; at least 30 rows before reading (6·13); expectancy; the grade column; set against a card you already trade; one change at a time, with a date (8·11). | 6, 8 |
| 5 | Forward Test, Then Keep or Retire | 2 | Its own 30 sessions on the simulator, then live at the smallest size, then the ladder (8·12, 8·15). When a card is retired, and why that is a result. | 8 |
| 6 | Capstone — Your Card | 2 R | The learner writes their card on the plan sheet and runs it through ten replays inside the lesson; the result is shown only as their own count, with how many rows are still missing. Badge. | 6, 7, 8 |

**New terms (draft):** Backtest, Forward test, Curve fitting, Hindsight. The outline stage confirms them and checks none is already taught.

## Decided on 2026-10-05

The outline's three open points, decided when David took the recommendations:
- **One set of examples for every path.** The chapter teaches a method, not a setup, so its charts are drawn so the time frame is not the point. There is no path switch inside a shared chapter (`docs/level-files/`), and none is built for this. When the swing and day-trading paths are written, their review stages (`SWING-REVIEW`, `DAY-REVIEW`) check that the examples still read right after their Chapter 8.
- **The replay step needs no new screen.** Level 3 uses `chart-replay` as it is (`docs/ui/05-chart-questions-and-mistakes-round.md` §4.4), and the next screen is a `journal-row` (`docs/ui/04-question-types.md`) where the learner logs the trigger they just took or passed.
- **No Checkpoint.** The chapter has six levels and ends on the capstone, which is its test.

The practice tool that lets the learner keep testing their card after the chapter is `SIM-ACCOUNT`'s (item P-02, `docs/plan/10-phase-1-app-arena-build.md`).
